# Sundown: evidence summary for judges

Written for: hackathon judges and technical reviewers. Everything below is offline research on US equities (2010-2026) replaying real gaps between a stock's last regular close and its next regular open, plus one replay run in forge's in-process EVM (not a public chain). The gaps are a **conservative proxy** for the true oracle-blind exposure, not an on-chain measurement. Full tables, intervals and code: `research/CLAIMS.md`, `research/results/`, `docs/REPLAY_RESULTS.md`; reproduce with `make backtest m21 m22 m23` in `research/`.

What ships: a **static, through-the-cycle** stress rule (full-sample q99.5 gap per asset and window class, 0.5 % oracle buffer, 1 % safety buffer) for a boosted tier on **AAPL at 93 %**. SPY and AAPL at 90 % are not deployed as boosted (the cap does not bind, or binds by 65 bps on Weekend windows only). Framing: **session-aware LLTV, boosted weekday capacity, tighter weekend capacity**; it is a capacity policy, not loss prevention.

## Three headline claims

**1. At the limits used today, weekend and holiday gaps cost lenders almost nothing, so we do not claim to fix them.** Replaying 2018-2026 (including March 2020) at the highest LLTV in the wild, 86 %, window-gap bad debt is 2.5 bps/yr (95 % CI [0.4, 5.6], date-clustered) and 0.00 bps/yr at 77 %; the worst single window cost 0.8 % of debt and 47 % of the 86 % loss is one episode. These levels use the deployed market's liquidation rule (M2.4); the older full-bonus convention gave 4.5 bps/yr [0.7, 9.7], an upper bound.

**2. The shipped rule does what its design says on AAPL at 93 %, and the numbers reconcile across two implementations.** In Session A's replay (forge in-process EVM; 20 seeded borrowers; the 10 worst real AAPL gaps; no borrower cures): the unprotected control at 93 % lost **$3,824 over 4 events**, the session-aware market **$1,224 over 1 event**, and a standard 86 % market **$0 at 7.5 % lower capacity**. The 2020-03-16 gap (13.9 %) **still produced a loss**: the cap is a q99.5 design point, not a worst-case bound. My independent Python simulation of the same scenario reproduces it to 0.01 % (control $3,823.47, session-aware $1,224.26) once it uses the market's liquidation rule; my earlier convention overstated the control by 45 % ($5,556), which is why both are reported. Over all 914 AAPL windows 2010-2026 (in-sample for the cap), the rule cuts bad debt at 93 % from 19.6 to 4.8-6.3 bps/yr: a reduction of 14.7 bps/yr (CI [3.2, 30.3]) for borrowers who never adjust and 13.2 (CI [2.2, 27.5]) for borrowers who trim before each window.

**3. Its economics are modest, and we say so.** SPY: the cap never binds, so a "boosted" SPY tier is simply a flat 90-93 % market with no protection. AAPL: the rule is **not distinguishable from running a flat market at the 89.35 % weekend-cap level** (rule minus flat-at-cap +2.7 bps/yr, CI [0.0, 8.2], at 93 % for attentive borrowers) and offers 3.65 pp of extra weekday capacity at 93 % (0.65 pp at 90 %). A borrower who never adjusts and sits near the maximum is deleveraged on 96 % of windows (52 times a year) at a 2 % fee: 4.3 % of debt per year on average and 11.8 % for borrowers clustered near the limit, against a **measured** AAPL borrow APR of 7.83 % (99.99 % utilised; on-chain read, Morpho Blue on Robinhood Chain, block 78,552,299). Attentive borrowers pay no fee but must repay to the cap before every window. Applied to a cap calibrated before March 2020 (out of sample), the rule does nothing at all.

Context (demand): the AAPL and NVDA stock-collateral markets are 99.99 % and 98.5 % utilised at 7.83 % and 10.92 % borrow APR; the SPY market is idle (42 % utilised, 0.06 % APR).

## What we will NOT claim

- That Sundown is "safer than Aave or Morpho", or that it protects conventional LLTV markets or prevents losses. The 2020-03-16 gap still produced a loss, and the benefit disappears out of sample.
- Any enforcement, protection or benefit for SPY at either tier or for AAPL at 90 %; anything for TSLA or NVDA beyond "not recommended for a boosted tier".
- That the shipped rule is safer than a flat market at the weekend-cap level, or that the earlier time-varying estimator's results (+2.6 pp LTV, 41 % bad-debt reduction) describe it: those belong to a research estimator that is not shipped.
- That naive borrowers are good customers (repeated forced deleveraging), that attentive borrowers have no cost (they must self-manage), that a keeper will run, or that the boosted tier is profitable for lenders or borrowers.
- That our 99 % gap-VaR is calibrated: out of sample it realises 1.87 % exceedances on weekends (target 1 %, CI [0.96, 3.09]); March 2020 breaks it.
- Any absolute bad-debt level as a forecast, any exact size of the oracle-blind exposure (daily proxy), or that exit liquidity is guaranteed (depth is read directly from Uniswap v3 pools, about $0.9M of collateral across four markets under the proposed cap rule, `docs/DISCOVERY.md` section g; no routing, RFQ or weekend measurement).
- That the replay ran on a public chain (it did not), or that any on-chain integration, issuer-risk mitigation or oracle security is validated by this research.
