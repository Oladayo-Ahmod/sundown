# M2.2 additions: liquidation design, exit liquidity, two-tier

Model (see `liquidation.py`): Uniswap-v3-in-range constant-product slippage `N/(N+y)`, `y = concentration x TVL / 2`, TVL from a **single secondary-source snapshot** (DexScreener, `pool_depth_snapshot.json`: TSLA $1.59M, NVDA $4.82M, AAPL $1.71M, SPY $7.68M); concentration 1 is the pessimistic anchor. Rational liquidators execute only while average slippage + 5 bps gas <= bonus; unexecuted demand is delayed to the next 30-minute round (pool refills fully between rounds, an assumption); price drifts along the same day's open-to-close return; leftover liquidatable positions are settled at the close with the design's bonus. Market debt = 0.25 x TVL (assumption; 0.1-1 tested). DEPLOY4 only (no depth data for the other assets). At infinite depth the simulator reproduces `credit_sim` to 1e-17 (test).

# M2.3: the SHIPPED static stress rule (SPY and AAPL at 90 % and 93 %)

**Rule evaluated (GUARD_DESIGN section 11, D18-D29).** From 6 h before a blind window until it ends, a boosted account's cap is `min(tierLLTV, 1 - gapVaR[class] - 0.5 % - 1 %)`, with `gapVaR[class]` the STATIC full-sample empirical q99.5 downside gap per asset and window class (`results/static_gapvar.csv`, provenance in the file; equals `class_stats.csv` column `loss_q99.5_bps`). Accounts above the stress fraction get a 3 h cure window, then anyone may deleverage them to cap - 0.5 % with fee `f` in [2 %, 5.5 %]. Standard-tier accounts are never touched, so only the boosted-tier book is simulated.

**Method.** `static_rule.py` (reuses the M2.2 liquidation model: convex depth-based slippage, liquidator participation constraint, 30-minute rounds, open-to-close drift, terminal settlement; flat 4 % bonus and the market's 50 % / 100 % close factor); depth = Session A's exact Uniswap v3 reads (`results/exit_liquidity.json`, `docs/DISCOVERY.md` section g), market scale = collateral cap 0.5 x notional sellable at 3 % (AAPL $98k, SPY $125k); 914 blind windows 2010-2026 per asset; `static_rule_analysis.py` writes `results/static_rule_*.csv`; intervals are 95 % window-date-clustered bootstraps (2,000 resamples, dates resampled jointly across SPY and AAPL). Verified by `tests/test_static_rule.py`, including exact equivalence to the earlier credit simulator at infinite depth. **The evaluation is in-sample for the cap** (the static gapVaR is a full-sample number that contains March 2020); S6 tests that.

## What we may claim about the shipped rule

- The rule is parameterised by a static, through-the-cycle gapVaR and, with those values, **never binds for SPY** and binds for AAPL only on Weekend windows (90 % tier) or Weekend and Long windows (93 % tier) (S1, S2).
- On AAPL, in-sample, it lowers lender bad debt compared with an unprotected flat market at the tier LLTV, by 1.65-17.88 bps/yr (uniform borrowers; more for borrowers clustered near the limit) depending on tier and borrower behaviour, with the intervals in S3.
- It buys 0.5-3.1 pp of LTV at equal lender bad debt (S4) and offers 3.65 pp (93 %) or 0.65 pp (90 %) more weekday capacity than a weekend-cap flat market, with the borrower costs in S5.
- Keeper economics are viable at demonstration scale under the 2 % default fee (S8).

## What we may NOT claim

- Any enforcement, protection or benefit for SPY at either tier; any claim for TSLA or NVDA beyond "not recommended for a boosted tier".
- Out-of-sample protection: the cap is calibrated on a sample that contains the stress it is evaluated on (S6).
- That the rule is safer than a flat market at the weekend cap level (it is not: S3), or that the boosted tier is a loss-prevention feature at real LLTVs.
- The time-varying results as the shipped rule: the +2.6 pp LTV gain (at counterfactual 93 %, 12 assets), the 41 % bad-debt reduction, the break-even APRs and the 'AAPL at 90 % headline' belong to the time-varying research estimator (rows tagged above).
- That naive borrowers are acceptable customers (S5), that rational borrowers have no cost (they must self-manage; their funds are assumed at hand), that a keeper exists, or that the boosted tier is profitable for lenders or borrowers (benefit to borrowers is application-specific and not estimated).

**Plain verdict: the shipped rule's economics are poor.** SPY: inert. AAPL: it binds, lowers bad debt against flat-at-tier, but is not safer than a flat market at the weekend cap, delivers 0.65-3.65 pp of extra weekday capacity, forces naive near-max borrowers into 45-52 deleverages a year (fees 1-12 % of debt a year) or forces attentive borrowers to self-trim every Friday, and its benefit disappears out of sample. Its defensible value is as a clearly labelled capacity feature for attentive AAPL borrowers, not as risk protection.
